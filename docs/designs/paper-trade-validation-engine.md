## Problem Statement

NEPSE traders already pay for signal/analysis subscriptions (NepseAlpha, Chukul, ShareAlpha, Mero Lagani, Share Sarathi, Stockyan, Share Hub), but no existing service has earned enough trust to be used alone — serious traders manually cross-verify across multiple paid apps before acting. arthasignal has the backend rigor (golden-test-verified signal detection, statistically-significance-tested backtests, survivorship-bias correction) to compete on substance, but has no product yet and no proof that this specific signal stack performs on live, forward, out-of-sample NEPSE data.

## Demand Evidence

- Founder's own first attempt at demand evidence was hypothetical ("everyone wants this," accuracy/price claims about a not-yet-built product) — acknowledged as such: "how can i use arthasignal already? i am just building it rn."
- Real evidence came from status quo, not direct usage: founder's uncle — a retired Nepal Army officer now working as a security guard, trading "in lakhs" across multiple brokerage accounts and applying for IPOs/FPOs — paid for NepseAlpha, complained it "gives wrong signals sometimes and it's too expensive," and **cancelled/switched away**. Real churn, not just complaint.
- Critical behavioral tell: the uncle now cross-verifies across multiple paid apps (Stockyan, Chukul, others — "he doesn't rely on 1 app") before trading. This is a manual trust-workaround that reveals the actual unmet need.

## Status Quo

Traders in this segment already pay for signal subscriptions and treat them as insufficiently trustworthy individually — enough that a security-guard-income user maintains multiple paid subscriptions simultaneously just to triangulate a decision. The cost isn't "no solution exists," it's redundant subscription spend plus manual cross-checking effort, with signals still sometimes wrong.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** Retail NEPSE traders who already pay for at least one signal service and have been burned by unverifiable accuracy claims — anchored on the founder's uncle as a concrete example (retired officer, security guard income, trades in lakhs across multiple accounts, applies to IPOs/FPOs, currently juggles 3+ paid apps).

**Narrowest wedge:** Not a better signal generator (every competitor already claims that). The wedge is a signal engine that grades its own calls publicly and automatically, before the outcome is known — the first NEPSE product where the track record is falsifiable rather than a marketing claim.

## Constraints

- Solo founder, pre-product, no existing users.
- Existing codebase already has signal computation (25 indicators/patterns, golden-test verified), statistical backtesting with significance testing and survivorship correction, fundamentals, corporate actions, IPO/FPO calendar, and daily/weekly automation via GitHub Actions + Supabase — no live/forward-test track record yet.
- Target customer segment is price-sensitive (security-guard income funding lakh-scale trades) — pricing must meaningfully undercut incumbents.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Private Paper-Trade Validation Engine (minimal viable — chosen first step)
Summary: Wire existing signal computation into a daily job logging every call (one call = one ticker+signal-type firing, entry price = next trading session's opening price) before the outcome is known, graded against market data at 1/5/20 **trading** days later (5-day horizon is primary/pass-fail; 1 and 20 day are informational only). Entirely private — no UI, no public claim.
Effort: S | Risk: Low
Pros: Fastest to build (new Supabase table + grading job on existing GH Actions pipeline); answers the one question that determines everything else (are these signals actually good, forward, out-of-sample) before any reputational bet is made.
Cons: No user-facing product during the validation window; zero revenue signal in this phase.
Reuses: signal computation, backtest framework, GH Actions automation, Supabase — all already built.

**Implementation decisions (from /plan-eng-review, cross-model-checked):**
- Calls are derived by a new `extract_signal_calls.py` script that reads the existing wide `TechnicalSignal` table and imports `run_signal_backtests.py::build_signal_conditions()` directly for signal definitions — never a second reimplementation of what counts as a signal firing. This function needs today's + yesterday's `TechnicalSignal` row plus `DailyPrice.close`, so the extraction script needs that same 3-way read.
- The extraction script MUST replicate `backtest_signals.py`'s episode dedup (`condition_active_by_symbol`, false→true transition = one call) — a condition staying true for 5 consecutive days is one call, not five. Without this, paper-trade calls are a different, autocorrelated statistical unit than the backtest episodes they're meant to validate against.
- Re-running the extraction job must not double-insert calls (unique constraint on symbol/date/signal_name, mirroring `TechnicalSignal`'s own `UniqueConstraint` pattern) — required test.
- Extraction must run strictly after next-day price ingestion in the daily pipeline ordering (entry price depends on it) — required test for this ordering.
- Minimum sample size: do NOT default to reusing `LOW_SAMPLE_THRESHOLD=500` from `compute_signal_confidence.py` — that number was calibrated against a 5-year, 287+35-symbol population. Before the validation starts, pull actual historical firing frequency per signal from existing `BacktestResult` data and set a per-signal-frequency-tier threshold (rare candlestick patterns need a different bar than common continuous-threshold signals like `rsi_14 < 30`), so the founder makes this call deliberately rather than inheriting a code convention that may make rare signals structurally unpassable regardless of quality.
- `grade_signal_calls.py` batches `DailyPrice` lookups by resolution date (matching `db_writers.py`'s bulk-upsert convention) rather than querying per call.

**Scope additions (from /plan-ceo-review, 2026-08-01, SELECTIVE EXPANSION cherry-pick):**
- Each `SignalCall` row is tagged with the git commit hash of `build_signal_conditions()` at the time it was created, so if signal logic changes mid-validation, old and new calls are never silently conflated in the hit-rate calculation.
- A weekly report (markdown) summarizing validation progress is generated and sent directly to the founder's uncle personally during the private phase — real human feedback before public launch, not just internal metrics. His contact info is stored as a secret/env var (same tier as DATABASE_URL/DISCORD_WEBHOOK_URL), never in the database — this is the first piece of personal data about a named individual this codebase would handle.

**Additional decisions (from /plan-ceo-review, 2026-08-01):**
- `extract_signal_calls.py`/`grade_signal_calls.py` are wrapped as non-critical in `run_all_daily.py` (log + degrade gracefully, matching the existing `signals_backfill`/`floorsheet` pattern), but with a distinct Discord alert on failure so a skipped validation day is visible rather than silently swallowed alongside routine non-critical failures.
- A job-level liveness check (in `data_quality.py`, which already runs daily) verifies the extraction/grading jobs actually produced output today — catches total job failure, which the per-call staleness check (TODOS.md) wouldn't catch if the job stops running entirely.

**Validation protocol (must be nailed down before the job starts running — see The Assignment):**
- Missing price on resolution day (thin/no-trade ticker): use next available trade price, capped at +3 trading days past the target horizon; if still no trade, mark the call VOID and exclude from hit-rate.
- Halted / circuit-breaker / delisted mid-window: mark VOID, exclude from hit-rate, log separately as a data-quality note (this is itself useful signal about NEPSE liquidity risk).
- Minimum sample size: pre-commit to a minimum number of graded calls (not just elapsed time) before the pass/fail bar is evaluated — if 4-6 weeks doesn't produce enough calls to be statistically meaningful, extend the window on a pre-committed schedule (e.g., +2 weeks), not ad hoc after seeing results.
- If the bar isn't cleared: do not launch Approach B. Re-examine which signal types are underperforming, retune or drop them, and re-run validation on the reduced set before trying again.

### Approach B: Public Trust Ledger + Telegram/Viber Channel (ideal architecture — sequenced after A clears the bar)
Summary: Once forward validation passes, launch a public channel where every signal posts live and auto-resolves to win/loss/pending (5-day horizon shown as the headline result; 1/20-day shown as supporting detail) — nothing quietly forgotten — plus a **static public scoreboard page** (not a full dashboard: no accounts, no login, no payments) showing cumulative win rate vs. buy-and-hold. A full web dashboard with accounts/payments is a separate, later build, only after the channel earns organic trust.
Effort: M | Risk: Medium
Pros: Directly targets the cross-verification pain (one trustworthy public source instead of four apps); distribution matches existing chat-app habits; natural path to a paid tier once trust exists.
Cons: Public misses are public — requires real discipline; a public channel needs moderation/anti-spam handling; the Telegram/Viber bot integration itself is new engineering (not reused from existing code) and needs its own small scoping pass once Approach A is running.
Reuses: grading job and signal engine from Approach A, existing pipeline automation.

### Approach C: Signal Fact-Checker / Verification Layer (creative/lateral — not selected, kept as future option)
Summary: Instead of building an 8th signal app, grade *other* providers' calls (NepseAlpha, Chukul, etc.) against a statistically-tested framework and publish an independent scorecard per competitor — automating the exact cross-verification behavior the uncle already does by hand.
Effort: L | Risk: High
Pros: Sidesteps direct competition with 7 named incumbents; matches observed user behavior directly; strong differentiation/attention angle.
Cons: Legal/reputational risk grading competitors publicly; requires ingesting their claims (scraping/manual entry friction); unclear monetization (who pays a referee?).

## Recommended Approach

**A → B, in sequence.** Approach A is a non-negotiable gate: the founder does not get to publish a track record built on backtests alone — it must be earned against live, out-of-sample NEPSE data first (4-6 weeks paper-trading). Once signals clear a real bar (beat coin-flip + fees, ideally with the existing significance-testing framework applied to the forward results), launch Approach B's public channel as the actual customer-facing product. Approach C is a genuinely interesting pivot but carries legal exposure and an unclear business model — worth revisiting only if A/B stalls or gets outcompeted head-on.

## Finalized Signal Scope (2026-08-01, resolves TODOS.md "check what existing backtests already show")

Pulled real numbers from `signal_confidence` and `backtest_results` before writing any extraction/grading code. Of 26 signals (not 25 — corrected count), 21 are already flagged `weak_or_no_edge`, `unreliable_low_sample`, `inconsistent_across_horizons`, `decayed_edge`, `liquidity_inverted`, or `unstable_multi_dimensional` and are excluded outright — no reason to spend the 4-6 week live window re-confirming what backtests already show.

Of the 5 remaining `high_confidence` signals, `shooting_star` is excluded as the raw signal: its own `cost_viability_note` states the edge goes negative once a realistic bid-ask spread is included ("do not treat as a confirmed tradeable edge"), and it only turns positive when MTF-confirmed. `rsi_14 > 70` had never had transaction-cost-viability analysis run (a gap in `compute_transaction_cost_adjusted_returns.py::TRACKED_SIGNALS`, now fixed) — re-run using the same methodology as the other signals, it clears costs at 10-day and 20-day horizons with the strongest 20-day margin of the four (+0.956% adjusted).

**Final 4-signal scope for the paper-trade validation:**

| signal | holding period | basis for inclusion | expected fires / month | 4-6wk window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rsi_14 < 30` (oversold) | 20d | robust across all liquidity tiers, MTF confirmation strengthens further, comfortable cost margin (+0.696% adjusted) | ~56 | ~56-84 |
| `close < bollinger_lower` | 20d | robust across all liquidity tiers, positive at all horizons; thin cost margin (+0.094%) — exactly the marginal case paper-trading should confirm before risking capital | ~108 | ~108-162 |
| `rsi_14 > 70` (overbought) | 10d or 20d | clears costs at both 10d (+0.329%) and 20d (+0.956%, strongest margin of the four) | ~61 | ~61-91 |
| `doji`, high-liquidity symbols only | 20d | liquidity-stratified backtest shows the edge is concentrated in high-liquidity names (avg +1.64 vs medium -0.17, low +0.59) — raw/unscoped signal not included | ~67 | ~67-100 |

Monthly firing rates are historical universe-wide averages (`backtest_results.sample_size` at the 5-day horizon, divided by the ~60-month backtest window, 2021-07-25 to 2026-07-30), restricted to the 89 high-liquidity symbols for `doji` specifically (of 269 classified symbols, split roughly into thirds by `symbol_liquidity_tier`). These are firing-frequency estimates for statistical design, not a claim that every live fire will be traded.
